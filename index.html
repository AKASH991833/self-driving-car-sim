Watch a car drive itself

A procedural track, a car with five ray-cast distance sensors, and a pure-pursuit autopilot that converts sensor readings into steering and throttle. Toggle sensor rays, switch to manual with the arrow keys, and try to beat the autopilot's lap.

How the autopilot works

Sense

Five rays project forward from the car (-60 to +60 degrees). Each ray returns the distance to the nearest wall - the same idea as a cheap lidar.

Think

A pure-pursuit controller picks a lookahead point on the track centerline, steered toward it with a PID-style correction. Wider clearance ahead means more throttle; tight walls mean brake.

Act

Steering angle and throttle apply to a simple bicycle-model car: position, heading and velocity integrated every frame at 60 fps.

Crash

Touch a wall and the car resets to the start line. The lap timer keeps your best - in both autopilot and manual mode.

Honest limits

  • The autopilot is a deterministic controller, not a learned policy - it follows the track centerline with sensor-guided speed control. It will not generalize to traffic, only to this track family.
  • The physics is a 2D bicycle model: no tire slip, weight transfer or suspension.
  • Manual mode needs a keyboard; on mobile, watch the autopilot.
